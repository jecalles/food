_A [[caramel]] made of 1 part butter : 1 part sugar, caramelized to a dark brown, and allowed to cool to a hard crack._
Status: #needs-practice 
Tags: [[baking]] 

# Ingredients
*For one full sheet pan (26"x18").*

_Essential_
- [ ] unsalted butter (450 g, 2 cup, 4 sticks)
- [ ] granulated sugar (450 g, 2 1/4 cup)
- [ ] water (60 mL, 4 tbsp)
- [ ] fine salt (4g, 1/2 tsp)

_Recommended_
- [ ] chocolate (340 g, 12 oz, roughly chopped)
- [ ] toasted nuts (150g - 200g, 1 cup - 1.5 cup, roughly chopped)
- [ ] flaky salt (for finishing)

_Optional_
- [ ] baking soda (3g, 1/2 tsp)

## Equipment
- candy thermometer
- full sheet pan (26"x18")
- oven (175C / 350F)
- heavy-bottomed saucepan, spatula or wooden spoon, and stove
- offset spatula

# Instructions
1. Prep: Line sheet pan with parchment paper. Measure out ingredients. Chop chocolate. 
2. Toast nuts at 175C / 350F for 8-10 minutes, stirring once, until fragrant and golden. Chop once cooled.
3. In a heavy-bottomed saucepan over medium heat, combine butter, sugar, water, and fine salt. Stir steadily and constantly until sugar dissolves.
4. Bring mixture to a full boil, then attach the candy thermometer. While steadily and constantly stirring, bring the mixture to 150C (300F). Mixture should take on a deep amber color and smell like toffee (10-15 min).
5. Take mixture off the heat, add baking soda, and quickly stir until just incorporated. Pour immediately into prepared sheet pan.
6. Allow toffee to partially cool and set (1-2 minutes). Scatter chopped chocolate evenly and allow the toffee's residual heat to melt it (2-3 minutes). Chocolate should soften and look visibly glossy.
7. Spread the chocolate with an offset spatula or spoon. Sprinkle nuts and flaky salt.
8. Allow toffee to cool fully. (1-2) hr / RT or (20-30) min / 4C.

# Notes
- Baking soda lightens the toffee and gives it a brittle consistency. Breaks more evenly without shattering. Does so by introducing bubbles that get trapped as the toffee hardens.
- Nuts burn quickly! Keep an eye on them as they get closer to done.
- When combining ingredients on the stove top, can I brown the butter first? Is that superfluous?
- Toffee, unlike caramel, **wants to be stirred constantly**. The butter helps the sugar from recrystallizing and becoming gritty, which is the primary fear when making a caramel.
- Sugar and butter mixture goes from frothy and pale -> thicker and darker -> peanut butter brown -> deep amber -> burnt. You're trying to catch it in the deep amber phase.
