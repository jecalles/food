# What's the concept?
Look, sometimes you've got tough, cartilaginous meat and you wanna make something unctuous. Proper ooey gooey yums. But also, you may not want to do a lot of planning here. You've got meat now, and you want food soon. Fear not, a [[braise]] is pretty gosh darned forgiving. Get your [[dutch-oven]]!
# The approach
**Meat**: connective tissue, something that won't tighten up with extended cooking. Chicken thighs and wings (NOT breast!), pork shoulder, ribs of all kinds, anything knuckle shaped, anything with a marrow-filled bone in the middle. You want gelatin / collagen to make a nice thick gravy of the braising liquid.

**Braising Liquid**: I think it should have acid, but not to break down the meat (it helps), but rather to balance against all the fat that will emulsify into the gravy. Wine (red for beef, white for chicken, but frankly either for either), tomato (sauce or crushed, hand crushed if you've got fresh), lemon or vinegar will do. Then, the remaining volume can come from stock (if you've got) or water (if not).

**Assembly**: (Optionally) brown the meat first (it comes out fine without this step). Next, [[sofrito-is-the-base]]. Pick your [[sofrito]] ingredients to fit the cultural archetype (or just to what's available in the fridge). Fry up [[flavor-paste]] (tomato is a good default; [[gochujang]] is a staple in the [[NuHaus]] fridge) and spices / hard herbs. Cover in braising liquid. I like to add some hard spices at this point too (whole black peppercorns is a vibe).

**Low and slow**: Oven at low temp (350F is a good default) is super set-and-forgettable. Good for a long, long braise, or if you want to stovetop space to do other stuff. Stovetop is also super reasonable (my preference). Bring to a boil, reduce to a bare simmer (on my stove, as low as I can go), cover, and peak-and-stir as you get curious. Stovetop is great for when you want to reduce to a target volume, when you want to make frequent adjustments (seasoning, additions, etc.), or if you don't know how long you need your braise to go.

**Rest your Meat!** Once you've hit time, remove your braise from heat and allow to rest, covered, for at least 20 minutes. Meat is muscles, and muscles tense up under heat.
