_A staple of Cuban food. Slow cooked black [[beans]], typically served over white rice as a side with almost *everything*. Commonly done as [[pressure-cook-beans]] in home kitchens . NOTE: this recipe is per pound (450 g) dry black beans; scale as needed._
Status: #in-practice 
Tags: [[cuban]]

# Ingredients

_Essential_
- [ ] black beans, dry (1 lb)
- [ ] yellow onion, diced (1x) and quartered (remaining)
- [ ] green pepper, diced (1/2 x) and quartered (remaining)
- [ ] garlic cloves, minced (4x cloves) and whole (remaining)
- [ ] olive oil
- [ ] vinegar (white or red wine)
- [ ] salt

_Recommended_
- [ ] bay leaf
- [ ] cumin
- [ ] bullion 
- [ ] vino seco 

_Optional_
- [ ] fresh cilantro or parsley, chopped
- [ ] lime, sliced in wedges

# Equipment
- Large bowl
- knife and cutting board
- wooden spoon and ladle
- [[pressure-cooker]] (stovetop preferable)
- saute pan and stovetop

# Instructions
1. The day before, sort and soak beans with salt overnight.
2. Soften beans in a [[pressure-cooker]].
	1. In a pressure cooker, add beans (drained) and cover with water.
	2. To flavor the water, add quartered onion and bell pepper, some cloves of garlic, bay leaf, and salt to taste. Optionally add bullion.
	3. Seal pressure cooker, bring to pressure over high heat, then reduce to medium low and cook for 20 min.
3. Meanwhile, prepare your [[sofrito]].
	1. Dice onion and bell pepper, and mince garlic.
	2. Saute in olive oil until fragrant and translucent.
	3. Add cumin and salt to taste. Optionally, add bullion powder.
	4. Optionally, deglaze with a splash of vino seco or cooking wine.
4. Combine beans and sofrito. Simmer over medium low with frequent stirring for another 20 minutes. 
5. Adjust final taste with salt and vinegar. Drizzle with olive oil liberally. Optionally, garnish with chopped cilantro.

# Notes
- Make beans the night before (saves prep time, and gets better in the fridge over time).
- Scale the cook time with the amount of beans cooked (e.g., 1 lb for 20 min, 2 lb for 25-30 min)
- Like all beans, finishing with acid is *essential*. Here, vinegar is typical. Lime wouldn't be unreasonable.
- Shaoxing Lao Chiew is a great substitute for vino seco. See [[food-substitutions]] for details
- These beans should be moderately mushy, with some whole beans for texture. If the beans aren't sufficiently broken apart, then you can do so after combining beans and sofrito. Crush the beans against the side of the pot as you stir.
