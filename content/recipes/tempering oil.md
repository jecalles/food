_Some cuisines (e.g., in [[Indian]]) will prepare a flavorful oil by extracting herbs, spices, and fresh aromatics into a hot, neutral oil (e.g., "tadka"). Typically poured over a dish to finish it._

_"What you put in first brings the most flavor" - Chef Sharma (BiBi, London)_
Status: 
Tags: [[cooking]]

# Ingredients

_Essential_
- [ ] neutral oil (e.g., ghee, vegetable, grapeseed)
- [ ] hard spices (e.g., coriander and cumin seeds, clove, star anise, green cardamom, black pepper)

_Recommended_
- [ ] aromatics (e.g., minced garlic, minced ginger, minced tumeric)
- [ ] fresh herb (e.g., cilantro)

# Equipment
- saucepan and stovetop
- wooden spoon or spatula

# Instructions
1. Over medium to medium high heat, bring oil to a shimmer.
2. Add hard spices, stir in, and cook until fragrant and / or popping (2 min). 
3. Add aromatics, stir in, and cook until fragrant (30 s).
4. Take the saucepan off the heat, add fresh herbs, and stir.

# Notes
- Don't take it too far! Most of the flavor is extracted right at the beginning. You run the risk of cooking out all the flavor.
- Green herbs should turn brighter green, but not go brown or dull. This is a sign that the cellular level structure has been disrupted (maybe?) but that the biomolecules (here: chlorophyl as indicator, but we care mostly about the aromatics) are intact.
- Yesterday (2026-04-04) Haus Cook, we made a chickpea and pumpkin curry. A _tadka_ of coriander and cumin seeds, clove, star anise, green cardamom, black pepper, and cilantro leaves elevated the dish dramatically!
