_Emulsification agent used as the basis for many a sauces. Core idea: one part flour to one part fat, cooked down. Typically uses butter (French) but other oils can be used (e.g., Cajun dark roux uses lard and drippings). The "darkness" of the roux refers to the color of the final product and depends on the duration of cooking._
Status: #needs-practice 
Tags: [[cooking-technique]] [[ratios]]

# Ingredients
_Core ratio (by weight): 1 part flour, 1 part fat. Can thicken between 8 ([[velouté]]) and 20 parts fluid ([[béchamel]])._

- [ ] flour (1 part)
- [ ] fat (butter, oil, fat from drippings, etc.) (1 part)
# Instructions
1. Melt fat in a sauce pan over medium-low to medium heat.
2. Add equal weight in flour and whisk to combine. 
3. With continuous stirring, cook out the flour until the taste of flour disappears.
4. Keep stirring and cook your roux to your desired color.

| Roux Type | Color | Approximate Cook Time | Typical Fat Used |
| ---------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| White | Pale ivory, no color change | 2–3 minutes | Butter |
| Blond | Light golden, straw-colored | 5–7 minutes | Butter |
| Brown | Deep caramel, copper-brown | 20–30 minutes | Oil or lard (butter solids burn) |
| Dark brown | Rich chocolate brown | 30–45 minutes | Oil, lard, or schmaltz |
| Cajun | Very dark brown, almost brick-red | 45–60+ minutes | Oil or lard |

# Notes
- The thickening power of a roux decreases with darker color and increased cook time. This must be because the thickening comes from polymerizing flour components, which get broken down and maillard-ed away into nothingness.
